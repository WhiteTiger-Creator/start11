# ledgerstore 1.9

Released 2024-01-15. **Current long-term support release.**

## Recovery

- **Placement of admitted unlinked segments.** An unlinked segment that passes
  the 1.7 admission check enters the tree at level 0. Its sequence number is
  assigned from the manifest checkpoint: the segments are ranked by ascending
  segment id and the segment at rank *r*, counting from zero, is given
  `checkpoint_seq + 1 + r`. Discarded segments take no rank and no number.

  The manifest the repair writes carries the checkpoint forward untouched:
  `checkpoint_seq` still names the last durable checkpoint and does **not**
  advance to the highest sequence number admission handed out, because admitting
  an unlinked segment does not itself take a checkpoint.
- The manifest entry the repair writes for an admitted segment describes the
  body it admitted: `records` is the number of body records in that segment,
  `bytes` is the sum of their `vb` values, and `min_key`/`max_key` are the
  smallest and largest keys it carries under the 1.6 byte collation.
- Every base row carries `version_count`: how many records for that key the
  merge considered across all admitted and linked segments, counting every
  record the key admissibility rule let through, not merely the one that won
  and not merely the distinct segments the key appeared in.

## Index

- **Shard boundaries follow stored bytes.** The base is divided into
  `shard_count` shards carrying as close to an equal share of stored value
  bytes as possible. Shard *k*, counting from one, closes at the first key at
  which the running total of stored bytes reaches `ceil(k * total /
  shard_count)`; the last shard takes whatever remains. Splitting on the key
  count instead produced shards that differed in size by two orders of
  magnitude.
- Each shard records its first and last key, its key count, its stored bytes
  and the deepest version chain it contains.

## Compaction planning

- **The level-0 plan is an exact optimum.** The planner selects a subset of the
  level-0 segments whose combined charged size does not exceed the configured
  merge budget and whose combined overlap score is the largest achievable. It
  is not a greedy or density-ordered walk: the planner must return the best
  score attainable within the budget, and a plan that scores below that
  optimum is a planning bug, not a heuristic.
- A segment's overlap score is the number of *other* level-0 segments whose
  key range intersects its own, endpoints included, measured after recovery so
  that admitted unlinked segments both count and are counted.
- Sizes are charged in whole mebibytes, rounded **up**, and a segment is always
  charged at least one.
- **The merge is bounded twice over.** Rewriting a segment costs the merge its
  bytes and it costs the merge its records, and the two do not track each other
  closely enough for one to stand in for the other. A plan must therefore fit
  BOTH the policy's `merge_budget_mib` and its `merge_record_budget`: a
  selection that fits the byte budget but rewrites more records than the record
  budget allows is not a plan, and neither is the reverse. Records are charged
  in whole thousands, rounded **up**, and a segment is always charged at least
  one thousand, so a segment carrying 5,747 records is charged 6,000 of the
  record budget. The optimum is the best score attainable inside both budgets
  at once; the best selection under the byte budget alone is usually not it.
- Where several selections reach the optimal score, the plan takes the one with
  the fewest segments; then the smallest charged size; then the smallest
  charged record count; then the lexicographically smallest list of segment
  ids.
- Only level 0 is planned. Level-1 and level-2 segments are not candidates.

## Merge output

- A segment body record carries four fields: `k`, the key; `op`, either `put`
  or `del`; `seq`, the sequence number stamped on that record when it was
  written; and `vb`, the stored size of its value in bytes.
- A row of the merged base names where its winning version came from. `level`
  and `segment` are the level and the id of the SEGMENT the winning record was
  read from. `seq` is the sequence number carried by the winning RECORD, not
  the sequence number of the segment holding it -- the segment's sequence
  number decides which record wins under the 1.5 precedence chain, and the
  record's own is what the base then reports. `key` and `value_bytes` are the
  winning record's `k` and `vb`.
